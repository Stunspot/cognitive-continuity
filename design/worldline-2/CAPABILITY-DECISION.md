# Worldline autobiographical rearchitecture: capability decision

Authority: stunspot, 2026-09-13, current task 01a09c51-1278-7293-9b9e-8d0584dc0500. The owner explicitly requested rebuilding Worldline and both Nova editions through Omnicompetence. This record preserves the design correction; it is not an inferred new product commission.

## Decision and diagnosis

Supersede Worldline's project-only center with an autobiographical occurrence timeline. Cognitive Continuity remains the canonical owner; Worldline remains a base Nova service, not an additional Faculty, persona, store, or autonomous authority. Keep the existing project-continuity interface as an explicitly named compatibility view. Confidence is high: the owner explained the intended use directly and the inspected implementation demonstrably requires one project/current-state selection.

The desired world-change is a persistent, navigable past that lets Nova recognize what happened, when, and where to find its substance. A Thing's occurrence is the record. Its conversation, document, repository, or artifact remains with its existing owner. Ordinary exploration belongs even without a decision, obligation, or deliverable. Projects are optional facets. Chronological awareness precedes selective detail retrieval.

The reusable deficit is both representation and behavior: project-key/current-validity filtering cannot represent a cross-project past, and an uninvoked read-only view cannot acquire that past. New occurrence records, temporal navigation, recognition-first projection, and meaningful capture/recall cues must ship together.

## Boundary and alternatives

A narrower prompt-only rewording is rejected: the existing query semantics would still lose projectless occurrences, links, and history. A broader universal transcript harvester, graph platform, background daemon, or new memory database is rejected: it duplicates custody and produces irrelevant volume. Adapt existing Continuity episode transactions and lifecycle; add exact occurrence metadata and a dedicated temporal projection.
